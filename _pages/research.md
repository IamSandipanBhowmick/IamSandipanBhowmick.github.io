## Research Overview

My research focuses on brain-inspired and neuromorphic computation from both applied and theoretical perspectives.

### Spiking Neural Networks for Medical Image Classification

I study lightweight spiking neural network architectures for medical image classification, especially brain tumor MRI classification and skin lesion analysis. My work emphasizes not only classification accuracy but also spike-rate behavior, timestep effects, membrane potential reset mechanisms, and energy-aware evaluation.

### Neuromorphic Computing

I am interested in neuromorphic computing as an alternative computational paradigm inspired by biological neural systems. My current work investigates how spiking activity, reset dynamics, and temporal simulation influence computational cost and deployability.

### Computability and Complexity Theory

My theoretical interests include computability theory, computational complexity theory, and their possible extensions to neuromorphic computation. I am particularly interested in abstract neuromorphic models, energy-bounded computation, decidability, and complexity classes for spike-based systems.
